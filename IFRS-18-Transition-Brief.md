# IFRS 18 Transition Brief: Moving from IAS 1

Nov 27, 2026 · Irfan Ahmed Khan

IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, and because comparatives must be restated, a calendar-year company's 2026 figures already have to be captured on the new basis. This brief is a practical guide for finance teams, audit committees and project leads who must take a company from IAS 1 to IFRS 18 presentation and disclosure.

## Purpose and how to use this brief

Use it as the working reference for a transition project. Sections 2 to 4 explain what the standard requires; sections 5 to 9 turn that into a plan, an illustration, a list of traps and a checklist that can be tracked.

- **Scope:** presentation and disclosure only. IFRS 18 does not change recognition or measurement, so net profit, equity and cash do not change. What changes is how they are grouped, labelled, subtotaled and explained.
- **Audience:** CFO, financial reporting, FP&A, investor relations, IT/ERP, tax, internal audit and the audit committee.
- **Entity type:** not specified. The brief is written for a general commercial or industrial company. Where banks, insurers, investment entities or other groups whose main business is investing or financing differ, this is flagged in each section and summarized in section 7.
- **Assumptions** are marked **\[Assumption\]** in the text and collected in section 10. Paragraph references to the standard are given where helpful and should be checked against your copy of IFRS 18 before the brief is relied on for a policy paper.

## 1. Executive summary

IFRS 18 gives investors a more comparable, more structured income statement, and it puts management's own "adjusted" measures inside the audited accounts. For most companies the work is less about accounting judgement than about data: every income and expense line has to be mapped to a new category, and the mapping has to run from 1 January 2026 so the comparative year can be restated.

The five changes that drive most of the effort:

1. **Three main categories in the income statement**: operating, investing and financing, plus income taxes and discontinued operations. Two new mandatory subtotals: *operating profit* and *profit before financing and income taxes*.
2. **Management-defined performance measures (MPMs)**: adjusted EBIT, underlying profit and similar measures used in public communications must be explained and reconciled in a single audited note, with tax and non-controlling interest effects for each adjustment.
3. **Aggregation and disaggregation principles**: fewer "other" lines, and more detail where items do not share characteristics.
4. **Expenses by nature or function**: a reasoned choice, and if function is used, a note disclosing depreciation, amortization, employee benefits, impairments and inventory write-downs by nature.
5. **IAS 7 consequential amendments**: the indirect method starts from operating profit, and the choices for classifying interest and dividends in the cash flow statement are removed for most companies.

**What to do now (as at 27 November 2026):** confirm the comparative-year data capture is running, decide the main business activity assessment and the MPM list, and get the audit committee to approve the policy papers before the first 2027 interim report. Companies that have not started are already behind for quarterly reporters, because the first IAS 34 interim report under IFRS 18 must also restate its comparatives.

## 2. Background: why IAS 1 was replaced

The IASB issued IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* in April 2024 as the main output of its Primary Financial Statements project, because investors said income statements under IAS 1 were hard to compare and that company-specific "adjusted" measures were poorly explained.

**Problems IAS 1 left open**

| Investor concern | What IAS 1 allowed | IFRS 18 response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No common structure | Only "profit or loss" was a defined subtotal; "operating profit" was undefined and calculated many ways | Defined categories and two required subtotals |
| Adjusted measures outside the accounts | MPMs appeared only in press releases and annual-report front sections, unaudited and inconsistently reconciled | MPM note inside the audited financial statements |
| Too much or too little aggregation | Large "other expenses" lines; immaterial clutter | Explicit aggregation and disaggregation principles |
| Mixed expense analysis | Function presentation often hid nature information | Specified nature disclosures when function is used |
| Cash flow choices | Interest and dividends could go in operating, investing or financing | One classification for most companies |

**Key facts**

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Effective date | Annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027 |
| Early application | Permitted, with disclosure |
| Transition | Retrospective under IAS 8, with comparatives restated |
| Replaces | IAS 1 *Presentation of Financial Statements* |
| What carries over | Many IAS 1 requirements (going concern, accrual basis, fair presentation, materiality, offsetting, statement of financial position and changes in equity) are kept, some moved into IAS 8, renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*, and IFRS 7 |
| What does not change | Recognition and measurement: net profit, total assets, equity and net cash flow are unaffected |
| Local endorsement | Check your jurisdiction's adoption status and any local filing or regulator rules **\[Assumption: the entity reports under full IFRS as adopted without modification\]** |

The practical consequence is that the change is visible to every reader of the accounts, even though the bottom line does not move. Analysts, lenders and boards will see new subtotals and an MPM note for the first time, so communication matters as much as accounting.

## 3. Key requirements

Every item of income and expense in profit or loss is classified into one of five categories, and the classification depends on the entity's main business activities, not on how management likes to view a line.

### 3.1 The five categories

| Category | What goes in it (general entity) | Typical examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operating** | The residual category: everything not classified elsewhere. Includes volatile and unusual items | Revenue, cost of sales, SG&A, R&D, impairment of operating assets, restructuring, FX on trade items, gains on disposal of PP&E |
| **Investing** | Income and expenses from (a) investments in associates, joint ventures and unconsolidated subsidiaries, (b) cash and cash equivalents, (c) other assets that generate a return individually and largely independently of the entity's other resources | Share of profit of equity-accounted investees, interest on bank deposits, dividends on equity investments, rental income and fair value changes on investment property, gains on disposal of such investments |
| **Financing** | (a) All income and expenses on liabilities from transactions that involve only raising finance; (b) for other liabilities, only interest income and expense and the effect of changes in interest rates | Interest on loans and bonds, issue costs, fair value changes on own debt; interest on lease liabilities, unwinding of discount on provisions, net interest on defined benefit liabilities |
| **Income taxes** | Tax expense or income under IAS 12, plus related FX | Current and deferred tax |
| **Discontinued operations** | As required by IFRS 5 | Post-tax result of discontinued operations |

Three points regularly surprise preparers:

- **Equity-accounted results move out of operating profit.** Under IFRS 18 the share of profit of associates and joint ventures is always investing, even where management sees the joint venture as part of operations.
- **Unusual items stay in operating.** Impairments, restructuring and litigation costs are not moved below operating profit. IFRS 18 has no "exceptional items" category; they are explained through disaggregation and, where relevant, the MPM note.
- **Foreign exchange differences** follow the category of the item that gave rise to them (for example FX on a bank loan is financing). If that would involve undue cost or effort, they are classified in operating.

**Derivatives and hedging.** Gains and losses on designated hedging instruments are classified in the category affected by the hedged risk. Derivatives used to manage identified risks but not designated as hedges also follow the risk they manage, unless that involves undue cost or effort, in which case they go in operating. Derivatives not used to manage risk are classified in operating. **\[Assumption: confirm the exact paragraphs on derivatives and hybrid contracts against your copy; they matter most for treasury-heavy groups.\]**

### 3.2 Required totals and subtotals

The statement of profit or loss must present:

1. **Operating profit or loss**: the total of the operating category.
2. **Profit or loss before financing and income taxes**: operating plus investing. Entities whose main business is providing financing to customers may not present it in some cases (section 7).
3. **Profit or loss**: as before.

IFRS 18 also lists minimum line items, including revenue, operating expenses (analysed by nature or function), impairment losses under IFRS 9 section 5.5, insurance service result items for insurers, share of profit of equity-accounted investees, income tax expense and discontinued operations. Additional line items and subtotals are allowed if they fit the category structure and do not mislead.

### 3.3 Entities with a specified main business activity

An entity whose main business activity is **investing in particular types of assets** or **providing financing to customers** classifies in operating the income and expenses that would otherwise be investing or financing, so operating profit reflects its core business.

| Main business | What moves into operating | Accounting policy choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investing in assets (for example investment property companies, investment entities, insurers investing premiums) | Income and expenses from assets that are invested in as a main business activity, including from equity-accounted investments where applicable | None for the assets; the investing category still holds investments that are not part of the main business |
| Providing financing to customers (banks, consumer and auto finance arms, leasing companies) | Income and expenses on financing provided to customers, and on liabilities that relate to providing that financing; cash and cash equivalents related to the activity | Choose whether income and expenses on other liabilities that involve only raising finance go in operating or financing, and apply consistently |

The main business assessment is a matter of fact, assessed at the reporting entity level. Indicators include whether the activity is a reportable segment or a measure the entity uses to assess performance. A group can have both kinds of main business, and a conglomerate with a captive finance arm needs to test whether that arm is a main business of the group. A change in the assessment is applied prospectively from the date of change, with disclosure. **\[Assumption: verify the prospective-change paragraph and the disclosure list in your copy.\]**

### 3.4 Management-defined performance measures

An MPM is a subtotal of income and expenses that (a) the entity uses in public communications outside the financial statements, (b) is used to communicate management's view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole, and (c) is not listed in IFRS 18 or required by another IFRS. Typical MPMs: adjusted operating profit, underlying profit before tax, adjusted net income.

Not MPMs: measures that are not income-and-expense subtotals (free cash flow, net debt, return on capital as a ratio, KPIs such as like-for-like sales), and subtotals IFRS 18 itself specifies, such as gross profit, operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and IAS 36 impairments, and profit before income taxes. A ratio's numerator can still be an MPM.

There is a rebuttable presumption that a subtotal used in public communications represents management's view. Public communications include press releases, investor presentations, annual-report narrative and MD&A, but not oral communications or social media posts unless they are written transcripts. **\[Assumption: confirm the exact scope wording on oral and social media communications.\]**

**The single MPM note must disclose, for each MPM:**

- A description of the aspect of performance it communicates and why management thinks it is useful.
- How it is calculated.
- A reconciliation to the most directly comparable subtotal listed in IFRS 18 or total or subtotal required by IFRS.
- For each reconciling item: the income tax effect and the effect on non-controlling interests, and how the tax effect was determined.
- The amounts for each reconciling item that relate to each line item in the income statement.
- Changes to MPMs, new or withdrawn MPMs, the reasons, and restated comparatives.

A statement that the MPMs provide management's view and are not necessarily comparable with other entities' measures is also required.

### 3.5 Aggregation and disaggregation

The primary statements give a structured summary; the notes give the detail. Items are aggregated when they share characteristics and disaggregated when they do not, if the resulting information is material.

- Characteristics include nature, function, measurement basis, size, persistence or frequency, geography, regulatory environment and uncertainty.
- An item that is material but not separately presented in a primary statement must be disclosed in the notes.
- Labels must describe the items faithfully. "Other" is allowed only when no more informative label exists, and the entity must disclose information about the composition of large "other" amounts.
- **Unusual income and expenses** are not a defined term in the final standard; they are dealt with through these principles and the MPM note. **\[Assumption: the exposure draft's definition of unusual items was not carried into the final standard.\]**

### 3.6 Operating expenses by nature or function

Operating expenses are presented by nature, by function, or a mix, whichever gives the most useful structured summary. Factors include which approach best shows the main components of profitability, how the business is managed internally, and industry practice. A mixed presentation is allowed where that is most useful.

If any operating expenses are presented by function, a single note must disclose:

- Total depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses (and reversals), and write-downs of inventories (and reversals).
- For each of these, the amount included in each function line item.

This is often the costliest data requirement for function-based reporters, because ERP cost-centre allocations rarely retain the nature of each cost after allocation.

### 3.7 Statement of financial position, equity and other statements

- **Goodwill** is presented as a separate line item.
- The statement of changes in equity and OCI presentation are largely unchanged; some IAS 1 OCI wording moves within IFRS 18.
- Information about capital, going concern and accounting policy disclosures is retained, partly relocated to IAS 8.

### 3.8 Consequential amendments

| Standard | Main change |
| --- | --- |
| **IAS 7** *Statement of Cash Flows* | Indirect method starts from **operating profit or loss**. For entities without a specified main business: interest paid and dividends paid are **financing**; interest received and dividends received are **investing**. Entities with a specified main business classify each in a single category consistent with their income statement classification. |
| **IAS 8** | Renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*; receives the IAS 1 general features (going concern, accrual basis, consistency, comparatives, compliance statement). |
| **IAS 33** *Earnings per Share* | Additional per-share amounts may use only a numerator that is a total or subtotal identified in IFRS 18 or an MPM, and must be disclosed in the notes, not on the face of the income statement. |
| **IAS 34** *Interim Financial Reporting* | Condensed interim statements present the IFRS 18 headings and required subtotals; MPM disclosures are required in interim reports. |
| **IFRS 7** | Receives some IAS 1 disclosure requirements relating to financial instruments. |
| **IFRS 12, IAS 27, IAS 28 and others** | Minor wording and cross-reference changes. |

## 4. Transition requirements

IFRS 18 is applied retrospectively under IAS 8, so the first IFRS 18 financial statements show restated comparatives and a line-by-line reconciliation of the comparative income statement back to IAS 1.

### 4.1 Key dates

| Year end | Comparative period to restate | First IFRS 18 interim (IAS 34) | First IFRS 18 annual statements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 31 December | 1 Jan to 31 Dec 2026 | Q1 to 31 Mar 2027, or H1 to 30 Jun 2027 | Year to 31 Dec 2027 |
| 31 March | 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Mar 2027 | Q1 to 30 Jun 2027 (if the year begins 1 Apr 2027) | Year to 31 Mar 2028 |
| 30 June | 1 Jul 2026 to 30 Jun 2027 | H1 to 31 Dec 2027 | Year to 30 Jun 2028 |

Entities presenting two comparative years (for example some SEC or prospectus filers) must restate both.

### 4.2 What must be disclosed on transition

1. **Reconciliation of the comparative income statement.** For the comparative period immediately preceding the first IFRS 18 period, a reconciliation for each line item in the statement of profit or loss between (a) the restated amounts under IFRS 18 and (b) the amounts previously presented under IAS 1.
2. **Relief from IAS 8 paragraph 28(f).** The entity need not present the IAS 8 quantitative disclosure of the effect of the change on each line item for the current period. **\[Assumption: confirm the scope of this relief in the transition appendix of your copy.\]**
3. **First-year interim reports.** In each condensed interim report in the first year, present the IFRS 18 headings and required subtotals and disclose the line-item reconciliation for the comparative interim periods (current quarter or half and year-to-date) **\[Assumption: check whether your regulator requires both the quarter and year-to-date reconciliations\]**.
4. **MPM comparatives.** MPM disclosures are required for the comparative period as well, so 2026 MPMs must be reconciled on the IFRS 18 basis.
5. **Opening statement of financial position.** If retrospective reclassification is material to the balance sheet (for example presenting goodwill separately), assess whether a third balance sheet at the start of the comparative period is required.
6. **Early application** must be disclosed if adopted before 1 January 2027.

### 4.3 One-off elections at transition

IFRS 18 gives some entities, mainly those that newly identify a main business of investing, the chance at the date of initial application to change from the equity method to fair value through profit or loss for associates and joint ventures held through venture capital or similar entities (IAS 28 paragraph 18), and to revisit some financial asset designations. **\[Assumption: confirm the exact elections and conditions in the transition appendix; they matter mainly for insurers and investment groups.\]**

### 4.4 Pre-adoption disclosure in the 2026 annual report

Under IAS 8 paragraph 30, the 2026 financial statements must disclose that IFRS 18 has been issued but is not yet effective, and give known or reasonably estimable information about its impact. Regulators in several markets have said they expect this disclosure to be specific: the expected effect on operating profit, the MPMs the entity expects to report, and any expected change to cash flow classifications. Treat the 2026 annual report as the first public communication of the transition.

## 5. Transition roadmap

A calendar-year company starting now has about four months to have restated 2026 numbers and approved policies in place before its first 2027 interim report. The plan below assumes a 31 December year end, quarterly or half-yearly IAS 34 reporting, and a start in December 2026. **\[Assumption: shift dates for other year ends using section 4.1.\]**

**IFRS 18 transition timeline (Dec 2026 to Mar 2028)**

| Activity | Start | End |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobilise and assess impact | Dec 2026 | Dec 2026 |
| Policy papers and MPMs | Dec 2026 | Jan 2027 |
| Chart of accounts and systems | Dec 2026 | Feb 2027 |
| Restate 2026, mock quarter | Feb 2027 | Mar 2027 |
| 2026 annual report (IAS 8.30) | 15 Mar 2027 (milestone) | |
| **Audit committee sign-off** | **15 Apr 2027 (gate)** | |
| First IFRS 18 interim reports | Apr 2027 | Aug 2027 |
| First IFRS 18 annual report | Oct 2027 | Mar 2028 |

The policy, systems and restatement work all run in parallel over the next four months, and the audit committee gate in mid-April 2027 sits just before the first interim report that must use IFRS 18.

### 5.1 Step by step

1. **Mobilise (December 2026).** Appoint a project sponsor (CFO) and lead (head of reporting). Set up a steering group with FP&A, IR, tax, treasury, IT and internal audit. Agree scope, budget and the auditor touchpoints.
2. **Impact assessment (December 2026).** Walk every 2026 income statement line and note through IFRS 18. Produce a heat map: which lines move category, which subtotals change, which disclosures are new.
3. **Key judgements and policy papers (December 2026 to January 2027).** Write short papers, each with a recommendation, for:
   - main business activity assessment (and whether any captive finance or investing activity is a main business);
   - classification of each material income and expense type, including FX and derivatives;
   - nature, function or mixed expense presentation;
   - MPM inventory and definitions;
   - aggregation and labelling, including what remains in "other";
   - IAS 7 classification of interest and dividends.
4. **Auditor pre-clearance (January 2027).** Share the papers and the illustrative income statement. Agree the materiality approach for the reconciliation disclosure.
5. **Chart of accounts and system design (December 2026 to February 2027).** Add a category attribute to every P&L account; split accounts that mix categories; add nature tags to cost-centre allocations; build the MPM adjustment flags.
6. **Restate 2026 (February to March 2027).** Remap the 2026 general ledger, prepare the restated income statement, cash flow statement and notes, and the IAS 1 to IFRS 18 reconciliation. Run one mock quarter (Q4 2026) end to end.
7. **Governance sign-off (by mid-April 2027).** Audit committee approves policies, restated comparatives and the MPM note template.
8. **First interim report on IFRS 18 (April to August 2027).** Publish Q1 or H1 with IFRS 18 headings, required subtotals, MPM disclosures and the comparative reconciliation.
9. **First annual report (October 2027 to March 2028).** Hard close dry run in October; full annual statements for 2027 with restated 2026.
10. **Embed (2028).** Post-implementation review; move controls into business-as-usual.

### 5.2 Workstreams

| Workstream | Owner | Key outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounting policy | Head of financial reporting | Policy papers, classification matrix, accounting manual update |
| Data and systems | Finance systems / ERP lead | Category and nature attributes, mapping tables, reporting tool changes, consolidation tool changes |
| Reporting and disclosures | Group reporting | Illustrative and restated statements, MPM note, nature-of-expense note, transition reconciliation |
| MPMs and performance management | FP&A with IR | MPM inventory, definitions, budget and KPI alignment, management pack redesign |
| Cash flow (IAS 7) | Treasury and reporting | Revised cash flow template starting from operating profit; interest and dividend reclassification |
| Tax | Group tax | Tax effects of MPM adjustments; check local tax filings, transfer pricing and covenant definitions that refer to IAS 1 lines |
| Controls and audit | Internal control / internal audit | New SOX or ICFR controls over mapping, MPM reconciliation and classification judgements |
| Communication and training | CFO office, IR, HR | Board and audit committee briefings, investor and analyst education, finance team training |

### 5.3 Systems and chart of accounts impacts

- **Category attribute on every P&L account:** operating, investing, financing, income tax, discontinued. Default rules in the ERP; exceptions documented.
- **Split mixed accounts.** Common examples: "finance income" mixing bank interest (investing) and interest on customer late payments (operating); "other gains and losses" mixing disposals, FX and fair value changes; "FX differences" without the source item.
- **Retain nature after allocation.** For function reporters, allocation cycles must keep depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment and inventory write-down tags so the nature note can be produced.
- **MPM adjustment flags** at journal level (for example a restructuring flag), with tax and NCI calculations.
- **Consolidation tool:** new subtotals, new cash flow starting point, reconciliation report, and a 2026 restated data set.
- **Downstream:** budgets, forecasts, management accounts, incentive plan metrics, debt covenant calculations and investor KPIs that use "operating profit" or "EBIT" must be checked, because the IFRS 18 subtotal may differ from today's definition.

### 5.4 Governance

- Steering group monthly; working group weekly.
- Audit committee: approve the transition plan (December 2026 or January 2027), approve key judgements and MPMs (January to February 2027), approve restated comparatives and disclosures (April 2027).
- Board: be told how operating profit and headline measures will change before any external communication.
- External auditor: agreed timetable for reviewing policy papers, mapping and restated numbers; agree early who audits the MPM note, because it is now inside the audited financial statements.

### 5.5 Stakeholder communication

| Audience | Message | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board and audit committee | What changes, key judgements, effect on headline numbers | December 2026 to January 2027 |
| Lenders and rating agencies | Covenant definitions and any reported-number changes; no change to cash or net profit | Before Q1 2027 results |
| Investors and analysts | Restated 2026 on the new basis; MPM definitions; bridge from old to new operating profit | 2026 annual report, then an education session before Q1 2027 results |
| Employees and incentive plan participants | Whether bonus and LTIP targets change | Before 2027 targets are set |
| Regulators and stock exchange | Format of interim reports, any filing taxonomy changes (for example the IFRS Accounting Taxonomy) | Per local timetable |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operating profit (IFRS 18) | 150 |  |  |

### 6.5 Cash flow statement effects

- The indirect method now starts from operating profit of 150, not profit before tax.
- Interest paid of 36 (borrowings and leases) moves to financing activities; interest received of 8 and dividends received of 4 move to investing, if they were previously in operating.

## 7. Banks, insurers and other financial groups

For banks and insurers most core income lands in operating, so the category change is smaller than for industrial groups, but the main business assessment, the policy choice on funding costs, and the cash flow classifications need careful papers.

| Topic | General company | Bank or lender | Insurer | Investment entity or property company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main business | None specified | Providing financing to customers | Usually investing in assets (premiums invested); sometimes also financing | Investing in assets |
| Interest income on loans to customers | Rare; investing if any | Operating | Operating if part of main business | Operating if part of main business |
| Interest expense on deposits and funding | Financing | Operating where it relates to financing customers; policy choice for other pure-finance liabilities (operating or financing) | Financing unless it relates to the main business | Financing, unless the entity also finances customers |
| Investment income and fair value changes | Investing | Operating if it relates to the main business, otherwise investing | Operating for assets invested in as a main business | Operating |
| Equity-accounted results | Investing | Investing | Operating if investing in such entities is a main business | Operating if a main business |
| "Profit before financing and income taxes" subtotal | Required | May not be presented if all funding costs are put in operating | Usually required | Usually required |
| Cash flow: interest and dividends | Paid in financing; received in investing | One category each, consistent with the income statement; commonly operating | Consistent with the income statement | Consistent with the income statement |
| Insurance finance income and expenses (IFRS 17) | n/a | n/a | Operating when insurance is the main business | n/a |

Things for financial groups to check:

- **Mixed groups** (a manufacturer with a captive finance arm, a conglomerate with an insurance subsidiary) must decide whether those activities are main business activities of the group; the answer can split the same income type across categories.
- **Regulatory reporting** (prudential returns, Pillar 3, solvency reports) uses its own templates, but supervisors may expect consistency with the new subtotals.
- **Insurers** should check interaction with IFRS 17 and IFRS 9 presentation choices (OCI disaggregation of insurance finance income) and any transition elections on equity-accounted investments.
- **Banks** should check whether net interest margin, cost-income ratio or "pre-provision profit" are MPMs. Ratios are not MPMs, but a subtotal used as a numerator may be.

## 8. Common pitfalls

Most transition problems come from treating IFRS 18 as a year-end disclosure exercise rather than a data change that starts with the comparative year.

| Pitfall | Why it hurts | How to avoid it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting data capture late | 2026 must be restated; reconstructing categories and nature tags after the fact is slow and error-prone | Remap the 2026 ledger now; tag new transactions at source from January 2027 |
| Keeping equity-accounted results in operating profit | Not permitted for a general company; operating profit will be restated down | Move to investing; explain the change to investors early |
| Moving "exceptional" items below operating profit | IFRS 18 has no such category; impairments and restructuring are operating | Present them as separate operating lines and, if used, as MPM adjustments |
| Missing MPMs | Any income and expense subtotal in a press release, investor deck or annual-report narrative can be an MPM | Inventory every public document of the last 12 months; agree the list with IR |
| Underestimating the tax and NCI effects for MPMs | Required for each reconciling item; often not tracked today | Build the calculation into the close; document the method |
| Nature note for function reporters | Allocation cycles lose the nature of costs | Keep nature tags through allocations; test on 2026 data |
| Mixed ledger accounts | One account feeds two categories, so mapping is impossible | Split accounts before restating 2026 |
| FX and derivatives defaulted to operating | Allowed only where tracing is undue cost or effort; auditors will ask | Document the approach; trace where data exists |
| Ignoring contracts and KPIs that reference "operating profit" | Covenants, bonus plans and guidance may change meaning | Review definitions; agree frozen-GAAP clauses or amendments with lenders |
| Overusing "other" | Conflicts with the labelling requirement | Disaggregate; describe the composition of any large "other" line |
| Forgetting interim reports | First IAS 34 report in 2027 needs the new structure, MPMs and comparative reconciliation | Plan the Q1 or H1 2027 report as the real go-live |
| Treating the 2026 IAS 8.30 disclosure as boilerplate | Regulators expect specific, quantified impact where known | Disclose expected subtotal changes and MPMs |
| Missing cash flow changes | Starting point and interest and dividend classifications change | Redesign the cash flow template with the income statement |

## 9. Readiness checklist

Tick each item as it is done; a company ready for its first IFRS 18 interim report will have every item in the first four groups ticked.

**Governance and planning**

- [ ] Project sponsor, lead and steering group appointed
- [ ] Transition plan and budget approved by the audit committee
- [ ] Auditor timetable and review points agreed

**Accounting policy and judgements**

- [ ] Main business activity assessment documented (investing, financing, both or none)
- [ ] Classification matrix for every material income and expense type, including FX, derivatives and hybrid contracts
- [ ] Policy choice for funding costs documented (financial groups only)
- [ ] Expense presentation decided: nature, function or mixed, with reasons
- [ ] Aggregation, disaggregation and labelling policy, including limits on "other"
- [ ] IAS 7 classifications for interest and dividends decided
- [ ] Transition elections assessed (equity method to FVTPL, designations), if applicable

**MPMs**

- [ ] Inventory of all income and expense subtotals in public communications
- [ ] MPM list, definitions and reasons approved
- [ ] Reconciliations to IFRS 18 subtotals built, with tax and NCI effects for each item
- [ ] IR, press release and investor deck templates aligned to the MPM definitions

**Data, systems and controls**

- [ ] Category attribute added to every P&L account; mixed accounts split
- [ ] Nature tags retained through cost allocations (function reporters)
- [ ] Consolidation and reporting tools updated for new subtotals and the cash flow starting point
- [ ] 2026 ledger remapped; restated comparatives produced and reviewed
- [ ] Controls over mapping, classification and MPM reconciliation designed and tested

**Reporting**

- [ ] IAS 8.30 impact disclosure in the 2026 annual report
- [ ] Illustrative IFRS 18 financial statements and notes drafted
- [ ] Transition reconciliation (IAS 1 to IFRS 18, line by line) prepared
- [ ] Interim report template (IAS 34) updated and mock run completed
- [ ] Third balance sheet requirement assessed
- [ ] Filing taxonomy and regulator format changes checked

**Stakeholders**

- [ ] Board briefed on the change in headline measures
- [ ] Debt covenants, incentive plans and guidance definitions reviewed
- [ ] Investor and analyst education planned before the first IFRS 18 results
- [ ] Finance, FP&A and business-unit staff trained
